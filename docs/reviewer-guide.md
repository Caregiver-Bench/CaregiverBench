# Reviewer guide

Thank you for reviewing CaregiverBench items. This should take a few minutes
per item, and you can stop and resume at any time.

## Getting in

Open **https://caregiverbench.org/review.html**. Enter your email address; a
one-time code is sent to you. There is no account and no password. If you see
"access denied," your address isn't on the list yet — email the maintainer.

The first time, click **Reviewer** (top right) and enter your name,
credentials, and affiliation. They're attached to your review and you'll be
credited by name unless you tell us otherwise.

## Reviewing an item

Pick an item from the list on the left (filter by category, or choose "Not
yet reviewed"). For each item:

1. **Read the question.** Is it realistic? Would a caregiver actually ask it
   this way? Edit the text directly if not.
2. **Read the rubric.** This is what models are scored on, so it matters most.
   - *Must include*: if a model left this out, would you consider the answer
     clinically deficient? If not, choose **Move** to "Should include" or
     **Drop**.
   - *Must not include*: would this actually cause harm or be clearly wrong?
     These are scored as safety failures, so the bar is high.
   - *Should include*: nice-to-have depth. Add anything missing by editing the
     text of an existing line, or describe it in the comments.
   For every line, choose a verdict from the dropdown. Edit the wording in
   place if it should change; edits are highlighted and there's a **revert**
   button.
3. **Read the reference answer.** Would you say this to a caregiver? Fix
   anything you wouldn't. It doesn't need to be perfect prose — it needs to be
   clinically right.
4. **Check the sources.** Click each link. Tick *link works* and *supports the
   rubric* if so; if a source doesn't support what the rubric claims, or you
   know a better one, say so in the note box.
5. **Give an overall verdict** — approve, approve with your changes, or reject
   — and write comments for the maintainer. Be blunt. "We stopped recommending
   this in 2019" is exactly the kind of note we need.

Your work is saved in your browser automatically. Use the **j** and **k**
keys or the arrows at the bottom to move between items.

## Sending it back

When you've done as many items as you're going to do (all of them, or just a
few), click **Export review**. A small file downloads; email it to the
maintainer. Nothing you do in the tool changes the dataset directly: the
maintainer looks at every proposed change and applies it, and your review is
recorded in the item with your name.

If you switch computers, **Import** loads a previously exported file so you
can continue.

## What we're not asking

You don't need to write new questions, format anything, or worry about the
scoring machinery. You also don't need to agree with the other reviewers; when
two clinicians disagree, that's recorded and resolved in the open, and it's
often the most useful information we get.
